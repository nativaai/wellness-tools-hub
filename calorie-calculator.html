Physiology

Components of Daily Energy Expenditure

1. Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): Represents 60% to 75% of daily energy expenditure in sedentary adults, sustaining heart rate, respiration, ion transport, and core temperature.

2. Thermic Effect of Food (TEF): Obligatory metabolic cost of digesting, absorbing, and assimilating nutrients (typically ~8% to 10% of total calories ingested).

3. Activity Thermogenesis (EAT & NEAT): Includes planned deliberate exercise (Exercise Activity Thermogenesis) and spontaneous non-exercise movements such as walking, posture, and fidgeting (Non-Exercise Activity Thermogenesis).

Research Validation

Why Mifflin-St Jeor Over Legacy Formulas?

Historically, the Harris-Benedict equation (formulated in 1919) was widely used. However, modern systematic reviews by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics demonstrated that Harris-Benedict overestimates resting energy expenditure by approximately 5% to 15% in contemporary populations.

The Mifflin-St Jeor formula was derived from 498 healthy men and women and was validated against indirect calorimetry as predicting resting energy expenditure within ±10% of measured values in over 82% of individuals.

Primary Academic Citation

Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST, Hill JO, Scott BJ, Daugherty SA, Koh YH. "A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals." The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition. 1990 Feb;51(2):241-7. doi: 10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241.

Formula: Men = 10W + 6.25H - 5A + 5 • Women = 10W + 6.25H - 5A - 161 • TDEE = BMR × PAL
