Neurobiology

What Causes "Sleep Inertia"?

Sleep inertia is the physiological state of impaired cognitive and sensory-motor performance immediately upon awakening. It occurs when an alarm abruptly disrupts deep slow-wave (N3 / delta) sleep.

During deep N3 sleep, cerebral blood flow and neural firing rates are markedly attenuated. Timing your awakening at the conclusion of a 90-minute ultradian cycle (during light N1 or N2 sleep) helps ensure prompt alertness and minimal grogginess.

Sleep Hygiene

Evidence-Based Sleep Hygiene Rules

1. Consistent Circadian Timing: Maintain anchor wake times even on weekends to synchronize the suprachiasmatic nucleus (the master circadian pacemaker).

2. Light Entrainment: Expose your eyes to outdoor sunlight within 30 minutes of waking to suppress daytime melatonin and promote evening sleep pressure.

3. Thermoregulatory Cooling: Core body temperature must drop by ~1°C to initiate sleep; keeping the bedroom cool (18°–20°C / 65°–68°F) facilitates this physiological cascade.
