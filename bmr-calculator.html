Physiological Definitions

BMR vs. RMR: The Clinical Distinction

Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR): Measured under strictly controlled laboratory circumstances: immediately upon waking after 8 hours of sleep, in a thermoneutral environment, after a 12-to-14 hour overnight fast, before any physical activity.

Resting Metabolic Rate (RMR): Measured under less restrictive resting conditions. Because it accounts for minor sympathetic arousal and residual digestive action, RMR is typically 3% to 10% higher than strict BMR.

Determinants

Biological Determinants of Metabolic Speed

1. Fat-Free Mass: Skeletal muscle and metabolically dense organs account for the majority of oxygen consumption. Increasing lean muscle mass elevates BMR over time.

2. Age Decline: BMR decreases by roughly 1% to 2% per decade after age 20, largely mediated by gradual sarcopenia (age-related muscle loss) and cellular mitochondrial slowing.

3. Endocrine Control: Thyroid hormones (triiodothyronine T3 and thyroxine T4) regulate cellular respiration rates across all human tissues.
