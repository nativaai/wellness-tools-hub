Clinical Context

Limitations & Clinical Nuance

1. Muscle vs. Adiposity: BMI measures weight relative to height; it does not distinguish between skeletal muscle, bone density, and subcutaneous or visceral fat. Resistance-trained individuals may present elevated BMIs without excess adipose tissue.

2. Demographic Variances: The WHO Western Pacific Region has proposed slightly lower cutoffs (23.0 for overweight, 27.5 for obesity) for certain Asian populations due to differential metabolic risk profiles.

3. Geriatric & Pediatric Notes: In adults over 65, slightly higher BMI values (23–27 kg/m²) are often considered clinically protective against frailty and sarcopenia.
